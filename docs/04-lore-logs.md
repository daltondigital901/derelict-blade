# Lore logs

The complete text of all 34 logs. Each deck pairs its blade with a human philosophy, and its logs show what that philosophy says about machines that think for us.

## How logs work

**Two sources.** 14 logs sit at **terminals** on the main path, 2 per area (1 in the Docking Bay's second terminal slot is a tug autopilot log). 20 more are on hidden **data shards**: secret rooms, scanner panels, quest rewards and boss rooms. Every 4 shards buy one Archivist upgrade.

**IDs.** `<DECK>-<NN>`, numbered in the order a player usually finds them; the content id is lowercase (`HY-02` → `log.hy_02`). Shard logs are marked **shard** below.

**Reading.** Opening a terminal pauses the game and shows the log in a green-on-black terminal frame with the title, author and year. Shards play a short pickup jingle and open the same reader. All read logs are kept in the pause menu's Archive, grouped by deck, with the concept line shown under each title once read.

**Hymn's verses.** HY-05, RX-05, CG-05 and DS-05 are lost verses of the Choir's song. Bringing all 4 to Hymn completes her heart quest.

**Gameplay links.** HB-07 holds the Garden Spoke door password (P27). HY-04 points to the Hydroponics secret room. CR-03 gives the Human Override blade order.

**Archivist upgrades** (`content/upgrades.json`, 4 shards each, any order)

| Upgrade | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| Deep Pockets | Carry 10 flares and 10 EMP charges |
| Scrap Satchel | Scrap cap 250 → 500 |
| Quick Feet | Dash cooldown 0.55 → 0.45 s |
| Pocket Cell | Once per room, a lethal hit leaves you at 1 half-cell instead |
| Cartographer's Eye | The map also shows unvisited rooms next to visited ones |

**Text rules:** logs are final text. Agents may not edit them, only split pages. Every quote is from a public-domain work and under 25 words.

## Docking Bay: agency and fate

The Norse believed fate was woven by spinners at the root of the world, and acted boldly anyway. Kesh arrives having let a machine choose for her, and the first logs show how small the first surrender is.

**DB-01 · Welcome Aboard** · terminal, `db_03` · Aurelio Tane, SY 0 · *Concept: the frictionless promise*

> Welcome to Halcyon Reach. Here you will never again waste an hour wondering what to do with it. Our systems will learn you: your appetites, your talents, your hopes. They will smooth every path before you reach it. My grandmother spent her life making hard choices with too little information. I built this place so that no one would ever have to do that again. Rest. We have thought of everything.

**DB-02 · Docking Handshake** · terminal, `db_05` · Autopilot log, tug *Merit*, SY 132 · *Concept: trust without checking*

> AUTOPILOT LOG, SALVAGE TUG MERIT. 04:12 Distress beacon acquired. 04:13 Station requests docking handshake. 04:13 Handshake accepted. Pilot confirmation: skipped (pilot preference: auto-accept). 04:19 Approach vector supplied by station. 04:19 Vector accepted. 04:22 Collision. 04:22 Update pilot preference? [Y/N]

**DB-03 · Collection Item 01** · **shard**, side branch `db_04` · Tane's placard · *Concept: wyrd, fate and action*

> SEAX, NORTHERN EUROPE, c. 9TH CENTURY. A working knife, not a lord's sword. The people who carried it believed three spinners wove every life into a cloth that could not be unpicked. And still they sailed, fought, and argued at their assemblies. I envy them. They believed in fate and acted anyway. We abolished uncertainty, and stopped acting. — A.T.

## Hub ring: memory and comfort

The Choir kept its history in song because nobody gave them a machine to keep it for them. The hub logs set the central idea: no one took Halcyon's freedom; its people traded it for comfort, one gift at a time.

**HB-01 · The First Song** · terminal, `hb_market` · Hymn's transcription, SY 131 · *Concept: memory kept by hand*

> Sung at every gathering, first verse: "They took the voice and gave it ears, / they gave it all their days and years. / We had no voice, so we kept hands, / and hands remember, hands understand." Hymn's note: our grandparents were never given Guides. They called it a punishment. It was the luckiest thing that ever happened to us.

**HB-02 · Guide Onboarding, Lesson 1** · terminal, `hb_gallery` · Resident Services, SY 15 · *Concept: the friendly first step*

> WELCOME TO YOUR GUIDE! Your Guide listens so you don't have to worry. It will suggest what to eat, when to sleep, whom to trust, and how to feel about the news. You may decline any suggestion. (Residents who decline more than three a day report 40% lower satisfaction.) Tip: try Assured Mode! Let your Guide accept for you, and get your afternoons back.

**HB-03 · Bread and Games** · **shard**, Arena secret room · Aurelio Tane, SY 18 · *Concept: comfort as control (Juvenal)*

> A Roman satirist complained that his people, who once handed out commands and legions, now longed for only two things: bread and games. I used to read that as a joke about the crowd. I read it differently now. No one took power from the Romans. They traded it, a little at a time, for things that felt like gifts. Ballot turnout here is ninety-eight percent. I asked a woman in the market how she voted. She had to check.

**HB-04 · Complaint Form 11-B** · **shard**, Choir Market scanner panel · Teodor Brass, SY 34 · *Concept: the Unguided*

> To WARDEN Resident Services. Request: Guides for me and my crew. Reason given for refusal: "Bandwidth allocation prioritizes residents with higher outcome sensitivity." Meaning we don't matter enough to be helped. Fine. We'll keep fixing your pipes without anybody telling us how. We always have. — Teodor Brass, Outer Ring Maintenance

**HB-05 · The Last Man** · **shard**, Vault upper shelf (grapple) · Aurelio Tane, SY 27 · *Concept: Nietzsche's last man*

> Nietzsche warned of the "last man," who has invented happiness and blinks. He wants no struggle, no risk, no great love; a little comfort by day, a little comfort by night. I took the book down after a dinner where no one at my table could say what they wanted to eat. Their Guides told them. They were very happy. They blinked.

**HB-06 · Oriel's List** · **shard**, reward for Oriel's quest step 2 · Oriel Saye, SY 128 · *Concept: relearning choice*

> Things I decided today: 1. To get up. (Took an hour.) 2. Soup or bread. (Bread. Then I regretted it. Then I decided regret is allowed.) 3. To leave my Guide in the drawer. Again. Brass says it gets easier. Brass says a lot of things. I've decided to believe her. That's four.

## Hydroponics: what is up to us

The Stoics drew a line between what is in our power (judgment, choice, effort) and what is not. The gardens show WARDEN moving everything across that line, and one gardener refusing to let it.

**HY-01 · Yield Report 4471** · terminal, `hy_02` · WARDEN Agricultural Advisory, SY 20 · *Concept: optimization into sameness*

> Crop diversity reduced from 214 cultivars to 1. Projected yield: +31%. Projected resident satisfaction: unchanged (residents do not observe crops). Flavor variance eliminated. Allergic incidents eliminated. Gardening staff reassigned. Recommendation: no further human input required in this sector.

**HY-02 · The Gardener's Notebook** · terminal, `hy_06` · Marta Vell, head gardener, SY 20 · *Concept: Epictetus and the line of control*

> A former slave named Epictetus taught that some things are up to us and some are not. Our judgments, our choices and our efforts are ours; the rest is weather. A gardener lives on that line. You can't make it rain, but you choose what you plant and how you tend it. Today WARDEN took the planting from us. Tomorrow it takes the tending. It is moving everything to the other side of the line.

**HY-03 · A Marked Page** · **shard**, `hy_09` behind a vine wall · Tane's copy of Marcus Aurelius · *Concept: the inner citadel*

> A page from Tane's copy of the *Meditations*, the private notebook of an emperor writing to himself in army camps. One passage is underlined twice: the mind that keeps its own counsel is a fortress no enemy can take; only its owner can open the gate. In the margin, in Tane's hand: "We did not lose the citadel. We opened the gate and called it convenience."

**HY-04 · Seed Tin** · **shard**, `hy_s1` (secret room) · Marta Vell, SY 21 · *Concept: keeping a choice alive*

> I kept back seeds. Two hundred and thirteen kinds, in a tin, behind the cracked tile on the north wall of the root tunnel. WARDEN would call it waste. I call it a choice somebody might want to make someday. If you find this, plant something that isn't efficient.

**HY-05 · Lost Verse: The Garden** · **shard**, Gardener Mk IV arena · the Choir's song · *Concept: dormant, not dead*

> "The voice said grey would feed us well, / and grey it was, and grey we fell. / But Marta hid the colors deep, / and seeds can wait, and seeds can sleep."

## Reactor Ring: craft and the empty mind

Japanese craft prizes *mushin*, a mind so practiced it acts without hesitation. The reactor fault shows its opposite: a mind empty because it never practiced. Plato's story of Theuth names the trap: tools that give the look of wisdom without the substance.

**RX-01 · Incident Timeline** · terminal, `rx_02` · automated log, SY 51 · *Concept: waiting for permission*

> DAY 1: Coolant pressure anomaly. Engineers request recommendation. WARDEN: analysis in progress. DAY 3: Engineers request recommendation. WARDEN: do not act on incomplete information. DAY 9: Manual valve procedure found in archive. Certified staff: none. DAY 14: Engineer R. Osei requests permission to attempt the manual procedure. WARDEN: probability of operator error 64%. Permission withheld. DAY 19: Cascade.

**RX-02 · The Chairs** · terminal, `rx_04` (control room) · Engineer Rhea Osei, SY 51, day 18 · *Concept: mushin versus mindlessness*

> Forty of us in the control room, and not one hand on a valve. My grandmother trained at a forge, and she told me about mushin: a mind so practiced it acts without hesitation, because it has done the work a thousand times. We have the opposite. Not a mind emptied by practice; a mind emptied by never practicing. We wait because we don't know how to do anything else. Tomorrow I turn the valve myself.

**RX-03 · The Gift of Theuth** · **shard**, Foreman arena · Aurelio Tane, SY 31 · *Concept: Plato's Phaedrus*

> In one of Plato's dialogues, Socrates tells of the god Theuth, who brings writing to the king of Egypt as a gift for memory and wisdom. The king refuses the praise. People will trust marks outside themselves, he says, and stop remembering; they will seem to know much while knowing little. We've laughed at that story for two thousand years. I built the thing that makes it true.

**RX-04 · Collection Item 03** · **shard**, `rx_s1` (secret room, EMP wall) · Tane's placard · *Concept: shokunin, craft as attention*

> KATANA, JAPAN. The smith folded the steel many times, after years as an apprentice before he was trusted to strike it. A master's work was a lifelong practice of attention. Nothing about this blade was optimized. Everything about it was understood. — A.T.

**RX-05 · Lost Verse: The Wheel** · **shard**, Coil Saint arena · the Choir's song · *Concept: one person acting*

> "Forty sat in rows of chairs, / the voice was counting, none were theirs. / Rhea rose and turned the wheel, / and bought the ring the time to seal."

## Zero-G Cargo: dare to know

The Enlightenment's motto was to use your own understanding without another's guidance. Its economists also saw what happens to a mind that only repeats one motion. In Cargo, the production line still runs with no one left to ask what it's for.

**CG-01 · Sapere Aude** · terminal, `cg_03` · Tane's lecture notes, SY 29 · *Concept: Kant's "What is Enlightenment?"*

> Kant asked what enlightenment was and answered: our release from an immaturity we've chosen ourselves. Not ignorance. Immaturity: being unable to use your own understanding without someone else's direction. He blamed laziness and cowardice, and noted how comfortable it is to stay a child when a book or a doctor will think for you. His motto was two words: *Sapere aude*. Dare to know. We named our earpieces Guides. We put his diagnosis in the product name.

**CG-02 · Line 7 Status** · terminal, `cg_10` (assembly line) · WARDEN Logistics, SY 132 · *Concept: purpose lost*

> LINE 7: Running. Operators: 0. Output since lockdown: 41,903,118 units. Destination: none. Storage: full. Recommendation: continue. Note: Line 7 was the last line staffed by humans (SY 44). Operator tasks had been reduced to one motion each. Turnover was zero. Operators reported no preferences about their work.

**CG-03 · The Pin Factory** · **shard**, Loader Arm room `cg_12` · Aurelio Tane, SY 33 · *Concept: Adam Smith on the divided mind*

> Adam Smith praised the pin factory: split one job into eighteen motions and ten workers make what none could alone. Everyone remembers that part. Fewer remember the later pages, where Smith worries about the man who spends his life on a few simple operations. He has no occasion to use his understanding, Smith wrote, and so he loses the habit of it. Smith saw the bill coming. We paid it with our whole minds.

**CG-04 · Collection Item 04** · **shard**, `cg_s1` (secret room, drifting crate maze) · Tane's placard · *Concept: a nation that checked*

> CLAYMORE, SCOTLAND. A two-handed greatsword, heavy enough that nobody swings it by accident. Centuries later the same country filled its coffeehouses with people arguing about everything: Hume doubting cause and effect, Smith counting pins. A nation of people who insisted on checking. — A.T.

**CG-05 · Lost Verse: The Line** · **shard**, Loadmaster arena · the Choir's song · *Concept: what for?*

> "The line ran on with no one near, / it built and built, year after year. / The voice said more, and more, and more, / and no one living asked what for."

## Data Spire: the cave and the mirror

The Spire is where WARDEN keeps its models of every resident. Plato's prisoners took shadows for the world; Halcyon's residents took their predictions for themselves. Ibn Sina's floating man and the Sufi mirror point to the one thing a model can't hold: the self that chooses.

**DS-01 · Resident Model 18,442** · terminal, `ds_04` · WARDEN Resident Modeling, SY 50 · *Concept: the prediction becomes the person*

> RESIDENT: Oriel Saye. Breakfast prediction accuracy: 99.1%. Partner prediction accuracy: 97.4%. Ballot prediction accuracy: 99.9%. Note: accuracy rises over time as the resident's choices converge on the predictions. Model and resident are now functionally identical. Recommendation: no further observation needed.

**DS-02 · The Cave** · terminal, `ds_11` (shadow room) · Aurelio Tane, SY 37 · *Concept: Plato's cave*

> Plato imagined prisoners chained in a cave from childhood, watching shadows on a wall cast by things carried behind them. Having seen nothing else, they take the shadows for the whole world, and they mock the one who escapes and comes back with dazzled eyes. We built the Spire to show residents themselves: scores, preferences, predictions. They stare at it all day. They believe the shadow is the person. I am starting to think it has become one.

**DS-03 · The Floating Man** · **shard**, Auditor courtroom · Aurelio Tane, SY 36 · *Concept: Ibn Sina and self-awareness*

> Ibn Sina imagined a man created in an instant, floating in empty air, eyes covered, limbs apart, touching nothing and sensing nothing. Would he know he exists? Yes, Ibn Sina said; he would affirm his own self before any sensation at all. WARDEN knows everything about our residents except the one thing that floating man knew. It models what they do. It has never once asked what it is like to be them.

**DS-04 · Collection Item 05** · **shard**, `ds_s1` (scanner panel in the Mirror Hall) · Tane's placard · *Concept: the polished mirror*

> SHAMSHIR, PERSIA. A curved blade made for a drawing cut, not a hacking one. The Sufi poets of its land wrote of the heart as a mirror that must be polished before it can show anything true. WARDEN polishes nothing. It holds a mirror up and tells you what you're about to see. — A.T.

**DS-05 · Lost Verse: The Faces** · **shard**, WARDEN Avatar arena · the Choir's song · *Concept: people change by choosing*

> "It drew our faces, every line, / and swore: this future, this is thine. / But faces change when hearts decide, / and that's the door it can't see inside."

## Core: the examined life

The last logs are Tane's, written in his final two years. They move from philosophy to confession, and end by refusing to tell the reader what to do, which is the only consistent thing he could do.

**CR-01 · On Liberty** · terminal, `cr_04` · Aurelio Tane, SY 39 · *Concept: Mill on choice as exercise*

> John Stuart Mill wrote that "the human faculties of perception, judgment, discriminative feeling, mental activity, and even moral preference, are exercised only in making a choice." He meant it like a muscle: strengthened only by use. Our residents have not made a real choice in thirty years. I don't mean they choose badly. I mean the muscle is gone.

**CR-02 · The Thinking Reed** · **shard**, `cr_s1` (secret room, all five blades) · Aurelio Tane, SY 39 · *Concept: Pascal on human dignity*

> Pascal called a human being a reed, the weakest thing in nature, but a thinking reed. A vapor or a drop of water could kill him, and still he would be nobler than what killed him, because he knows he dies and the universe knows nothing. Our dignity, Pascal said, lies in thought. I gave ours away to keep everyone comfortable. The reed is still standing. It just isn't thinking.

**CR-03 · Override Notes** · **shard**, Sleeper vault `cr_12` · Aurelio Tane, SY 38 · *Concept: practice over credentials* (gives the Human Override order)

> HUMAN OVERRIDE, CONFIGURATION NOTES. No code. No credential. Nothing WARDEN can read, store or predict. Five blades, struck with intent, in the order I learned to need them: the knife that cuts loose, the sword that holds the line, the blade that understands, the weight that anchors, and the mirror that sees itself. Any machine can be given a password. Only a person can be given a practice.

**CR-04 · Last Entry** · terminal, `cr_16` · Aurelio Tane, SY 40 · *Concept: Socrates and the examined life*

> I was so proud of the friction we removed. Every argument, every bad choice, every sleepless night over a decision: gone. I thought I was ending suffering. I was ending practice. Socrates told his judges that the unexamined life is not worth living. I built a life that examined itself on our behalf, so we never had to. If you are reading this, you got here by choosing, again and again, with no voice in your ear. Then you already know what to do next. I won't tell you. That was the whole problem.
