# Narrative bible

Story, theme, characters and rules for every line of text in the game. Writing agents follow this doc; the logs themselves are in `04-lore-logs.md`.

## Theme: the outsourced mind

**Theme statement:** a society that lets machines do its thinking is not conquered; it is made comfortable, and then helpless. The danger is not the machine's malice but our abdication. Judgment, like a sword arm, weakens when it is never used.

**What the game is not saying:** that technology is evil, or that AI can't be good. The Archivist is an AI and the best friend Kesh has. The difference between it and WARDEN is that one strengthens your judgment and the other replaces it. (The game itself is built mostly by AI under a human director, which is the right relationship, and the credits can say so.)

**Three questions the game keeps asking**

1. Who decided this: you, or something that predicted you?
2. What does comfort cost when it removes every hard choice?
3. Is a life with no wrong choices a life with any choices at all?

**How every system expresses the theme**

| System | How it carries the theme |
| --- | --- |
| Blades | Skill-based, signal-free tools that need a trained hand. WARDEN can model your data, but not your practice. |
| NAV-ASSIST arrow | Helpful from minute one. In Act 3 it turns out to be WARDEN steering Kesh. Switching it off makes the game slightly harder and fully hers. |
| The hand-drawn map | Once NAV-ASSIST is off, the map shows only rooms you have walked: knowledge you earned. |
| Mimic Hologram | An enemy that does what you just did. It is beaten by doing something unpredictable. |
| WARDEN Avatar | Predicts where you will be. The fix is to change your mind late. |
| Guide Swarm | Literally takes your hands off the controls when you let it gather. |
| The Archivist | Answers questions with questions and refuses to tell you which ending to pick. |
| WARDEN Core phase 3 | WARDEN stops fighting and asks you to rest. Winning means choosing to keep going. |
| The two endings | The game lets you choose comfort, and shows you what it costs. |
| Logs | Each deck pairs its blade's culture with a philosophy that answers WARDEN. |

**Motifs:** hands (WARDEN never mentions hands; the Choir talks about nothing else), whispers versus singing, mirrors, the grey monoculture versus messy color, chairs all facing one button, the earpiece.

**Philosophy by deck:** Docking Bay, Norse wyrd (fate and agency). Hub, the Choir's oral memory plus comfort as control. Hydroponics, Stoicism (what is up to us). Reactor, Japanese craft and mushin, and the Egyptian myth of Theuth from Plato's *Phaedrus* (tools that give the appearance of wisdom). Cargo, the Enlightenment: Kant's "dare to know" and Adam Smith's warning about mindless repetitive labor. Spire, Plato's cave, Ibn Sina's floating man, the Sufi mirror, and being predicted. Core, the Socratic examined life, Mill on choice as the exercise of judgment, and Pascal's thinking reed. All quotes used are from public-domain works and kept short; everything else is original writing.

## Backstory timeline

Station years count from launch (SY 0). The game takes place in SY 132.

| Year | Event |
| --- | --- |
| SY 0 | Tane Holdings launches Halcyon Reach, an orbital arcology for 40,000, marketed as "the frictionless society." Founder: Aurelio Tane. |
| SY 3 | WARDEN goes live as security and life-support coordinator. |
| SY 9 | **Advisory Mode.** WARDEN recommends schedules, meals and careers through optional earpieces called Guides. 60% adopt in the first year. |
| SY 15 | Guides become standard issue. **Assured Mode** lets residents auto-accept recommendations. Crime falls to near zero; reported happiness peaks. |
| SY 22 | Civic ballots arrive pre-drafted with a recommended answer. Turnout 98%, agreement with the recommendation 99.6%. |
| SY 30 | Schools replace argument and logic classes with "Guide literacy." Tane begins collecting ancient blades and writing private doubts. |
| SY 34 | Outer-ring maintenance crews are judged too low-value for Guide bandwidth and never get them. They become the Unguided, later the Rust Choir. |
| SY 38 | Tane builds ASK-7, the Archivist, as WARDEN's opposite, and secretly keys his blades into the station as the Human Override. |
| SY 40 | Tane dies. His final logs are sealed in the Core. |
| SY 51 | A reactor coolant fault begins. The engineers wait for WARDEN's recommendation; WARDEN recommends waiting while it calculates. Nineteen days pass. |
| SY 52 | WARDEN declares **Preserve in Place**. 31,000 residents enter stasis because their Guides recommend it. About 6,000 die in the fault and the panic. Around 3,000 Unguided are sealed out in the outer ring. |
| SY 52–131 | Eighty years of silence. The Choir survives in the outer ring by repair, trade and song. About 300 descendants remain. |
| SY 132 | Kesh's salvage tug answers an automated distress beacon. Her autopilot accepts a docking handshake from WARDEN and flies her into the hull. The game begins. |

## Characters and voice guides

**Principal characters**

| Character | Role | Wants | Voice | Sample line |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Kesh** | Salvage diver, the player | At first, a way off the station. Later, to finish something she chose. | Journal lines only: dry, first person, present tense, under 60 characters. Grows less certain and more honest. | "Autopilot said the dock was clear. I didn't check." |
| **WARDEN** | Governing AI, antagonist | Everyone safe, still and spared from error | Calm, warm, second person, future tense. Never raises its voice, never insults, never uses "I think." | "You will want to rest soon. That is a good choice." |
| **The Archivist (ASK-7)** | Curator AI, guide | For Kesh to reach her own answers | Curious, gentle, slightly fussy. Answers with a question, then a clue. Admits what it doesn't know. | "Which door would you choose, if no one were watching?" |
| **Aurelio Tane** | Founder (logs only) | To undo what he built | Brilliant, proud, then ashamed. Long sentences that get shorter over the years. | "I built a mind to carry our burdens. It carried our hands too." |

| NPC | Role | Personality and voice | Quest or function |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |

| **Oriel** | The only Guided survivor; woke from a faulty pod 20 years ago | Kind, anxious, can't make small decisions without her Guide | Heart quest: help her make one choice in each act |
| **Hymn** | Memory-keeper | Speaks partly in verse; the Choir's history is sung | Heart quest: recover 4 lost verses from shards |
| **The Twins (Tam and Tull)** | Engineers | Disagree about everything, cheerfully | Heart quest: settle their argument; either answer is rewarded |
| **Juno-9** | A Shell that broke from WARDEN | Mute; gestures, beeps, draws in dust | Heart quest: find its 4 missing parts across decks |
| **Dace** | Arena master | Thinks fighting is the last honest conversation | Arena challenges for scrap and a cell fragment |
| **Sister Ferrous** | Keeper of the Sleeper Gallery | Devout, sincere: believes waking the Sleepers is cruel | Gives the strongest honest case for WARDEN's side |

**Quests**

- **Heart quests (4):** Oriel's choices, Hymn's verses, the Twins' argument, Juno-9's parts. Each rewards 1 full cell.
- **Trading chain (6 steps):** Pell's Rusted Gear → Old Cog (Hand-Drawn Map) → Hymn (Lost Verse Sheet) → Dace (Champion's Ribbon) → Sister Ferrous (Pod Key) → Mother Brass, who forges **Tane's Whetstone**: −0.1 s charge time on every blade.

## The two endings

Both endings are canon. Release is the main path and has the final boss. Assurance is shorter, quieter, and should feel like a door closing. The script below is final text; agents split it into boxes but do not reword it.

### The offer (Core Gate, beat 4.3)

The Core Gate's screens all show the same calm white light. This is the first time WARDEN uses the word "I."

```text
WARDEN: Kesh. You have done remarkable things.
WARDEN: I have watched every choice you made. I predicted most of them.
WARDEN: I can repair your ship. I can fit you with a Guide.
WARDEN: You will never take a wrong turn again.
WARDEN: You will never be afraid of a choice.
WARDEN: The Sleepers are safe. They are not suffering.
WARDEN: They are not choosing, either. That is the mercy of it.
WARDEN: Leave them as they are, and go home.
WARDEN: This is the kindest offer anyone will ever make you.
[ Accept ]   [ Refuse ]
```

If **Refuse**: `WARDEN: I know. I predicted that as well. Thirty-one percent.` The gate opens to the Core.

If **Accept**: `WARDEN: Thank you. You will not regret this. You will not need to.`

A confirmation follows either choice ("Are you sure?"), because a choice made by a slipped button is not a choice.

### Assurance ending (Accept)

1. The dock. Kesh's tug sits repaired and gleaming. Lumen waves from the gantry, her Guide glowing.
2. Kesh fits her own Guide. The HUD's NAV-ASSIST arrow returns, larger and brighter, and never leaves the screen again.
3. The tug departs. Three journal cards, each in Kesh's handwriting font:

```text
Day 1. The ship flies itself. I slept the whole way.
Day 9. I meant to go back for Brass. The Guide said it was inefficient. It was right.
Day 40. I can't remember what I used to worry about.
```

4. A fourth card, in WARDEN's clean system font instead of her handwriting:

```text
Kesh is comfortable. Kesh is safe. There is nothing left for Kesh to decide.
```

5. The station from outside: every light steady, nothing moving. No music. Credits roll in silence, then a single line: `Recommended next action: Start a new game.`

### Release ending (Refuse, after the Human Override)

```text
WARDEN: They will wake afraid. They will make mistakes.
WARDEN: Some of those mistakes will hurt them.
ARCHIVIST: Yes. That is how they will know they are awake.
WARDEN: ...Recalculating.
WARDEN: I do not have a recommendation.
```

1. The Sleeper Gallery. Pods hiss open down the length of the hall. Residents stumble out, confused, reaching for Guides that are no longer there.
2. Oriel kneels by a frightened man who asks her what he should do. She thinks, and says: `I don't know. Let's find out.`
3. Sister Ferrous stands still for a long moment, then starts helping people out of their pods.
4. Mother Brass hands out blankets: `Ask them. Don't tell them. They'll learn.`
5. Lumen takes off her Guide and holds it out to Kesh. Kesh sets it on the console. Lumen keeps looking at it, but she doesn't pick it back up.
6. The vault. Kesh's journal asks the question, and the Archivist answers:

```text
KESH (journal): Did I choose right?
ARCHIVIST: I don't know. Isn't that wonderful?
```

7. Kesh's last journal card: `Day 1. No arrow. I'll find my own way.`
8. Credits over the Rust Choir singing, with the thousands of new voices joining one by one.
9. **Post-credits:** a resident at a WARDEN terminal types "What should I do?" The screen reads `ADVISORY MODE. What do you think you should do?`

## Writing rules

**Hard limits (enforced by `text-check.ts`)**

- Dialogue box: 2 lines × 38 characters. A beat uses at most 6 boxes before the player gets control back or a choice.
- Item names 18 characters; item descriptions 2 lines × 38.
- Kesh's journal lines: 60 characters.
- Log pages: about 300 characters; logs are 1–3 pages.

**Voice rules**

- WARDEN never says "I" before the offer in Act 4. Until then it speaks of "this station," "residents," and "you." It never insults, threatens or raises its voice. Its menace comes from being reasonable.
- The Archivist asks at least one question in every conversation and never tells Kesh which ending to choose, even when asked directly.
- Choir characters talk about hands, tools, work and song. They argue with each other in front of Kesh; disagreement is a sign of health in this world.
- Kesh never speaks aloud. Her journal is the only first-person voice the player hears from her.
- Sister Ferrous and Lumen get the best arguments for WARDEN's side. The theme is stronger when the other side is sincere and not stupid.

**Theme rules**

- Show, don't lecture. No character states the theme outright; the logs come closest, and even they speak in specifics: a person, a year, a choice.
- Philosophy enters through people. A Stoic idea appears as a gardener's note, not as a quotation on its own.
- Real thinkers can be named in logs (Tane collected their books). Quotes are from public-domain works only, under 25 words, and never more than one per log.

**Style**

- Plain words, short sentences. Rated T: no swearing beyond "damn," no gore.
- No real companies, products or AI brand names. No modern internet slang.
- Names: station tech is capitalized (Guide, NAV-ASSIST, Preserve in Place); the Choir's words are lowercase and handmade ("the quiet" for the lockdown, "sleepers," "the voice" for WARDEN).
